These are some of the books about **Iran, Iranian culture, literature and art** displayed at the **Iran table** during **World’s Day (Maailmanpäivä)** at **Alma in Vaasa on Saturday, 7 November 2026**. You can search for these titles in the [Vaasa City Library catalogue](https://vaasankirjasto.finna.fi/) and, where available, borrow or reserve them using your library card.

## Books on display

Each link opens a title search in Vaasa City Library’s catalogue. Availability and reservation options may change.

{{< iran-book title="Kuninkaiden kirja" img="img/blog/books/kuninkaiden-kirja.jpg" query="Kuninkaiden kirja Jaakko Hämeen-Anttila" label="Search Vaasa Library and check reservations" >}}

Ferdowsi’s *Shahnameh* (*Book of Kings*) is one of the great epics of Persian literature, telling the stories of ancient Iranian kings, legendary heroes and myths. This Finnish translation by Jaakko Hämeen-Anttila, illustrated by Heli Hieta, presents the early mythological part of the epic.

{{< /iran-book >}}

{{< iran-book title="Omar Khaijam: Runoilijan elämä" img="img/blog/books/omar-khaijam.jpg" query="Omar Khaijam Runoilijan elämä" label="Search Vaasa Library and check reservations" >}}

Jaakko Hämeen-Anttila’s biography introduces Omar Khayyam, the Iranian poet, mathematician, astronomer and thinker of the 11th and 12th centuries. The quatrains associated with Khayyam explore time, mortality and the value of the present moment.

{{< /iran-book >}}

{{< iran-book title="Den blinda ugglan" img="img/blog/books/den-blinda-ugglan.jpg" query="Den blinda ugglan Sadeq Hedayat" label="Search Vaasa Library and check reservations" >}}

*The Blind Owl* by Sadegh Hedayat is a landmark of modern Iranian fiction. This dark, dreamlike psychological novel explores isolation, anxiety and the blurred boundary between reality and imagination. The displayed edition is Bo Utas’s Swedish translation.

{{< /iran-book >}}

{{< iran-book title="Hafis: Offenbares Geheimnis" img="img/blog/books/hafis-offenbares-geheimnis.jpg" query="Hafis Offenbares Geheimnis" label="Search Vaasa Library and check reservations" >}}

This German selection introduces the poetry of Hafez of Shiraz, the celebrated 14th-century Persian poet. His ghazals weave together love, beauty, spirituality and criticism of hypocrisy. Hafez also inspired the German poet Goethe.

{{< /iran-book >}}

{{< iran-book title="Hafizin runoja" img="img/blog/books/hafizin-runoja.jpg" query="Hafizin runoja Henri Broms" label="Search Vaasa Library and check reservations" >}}

A Finnish selection of Hafez’s poetry translated by Henri Broms. Hafez remains a living presence in Iranian culture: his poems are read at celebrations and family gatherings, and his collected poetry is used in the tradition of *Fal-e Hafez*.

{{< /iran-book >}}

{{< iran-book title="Todellisuuden maailmat" img="img/blog/books/todellisuuden-maailmat.jpg" query="Todellisuuden maailmat Hämeen-Anttila" label="Search Vaasa Library and check reservations" >}}

Edited and translated by Jaakko Hämeen-Anttila, this collection brings together poetry and prose from Islamic mystics. The Persian and Arabic texts explore spiritual love, self-knowledge and humanity’s relationship with God.

{{< /iran-book >}}

{{< iran-book title="Humalan ja lemmen lauluja" img="img/blog/books/humalan-ja-lemmen-lauluja.jpg" query="Humalan ja lemmen lauluja Rumi" label="Search Vaasa Library and check reservations" >}}

A Finnish translation of quatrains by Jalal al-Din Rumi, translated by Joonas Maristo. Rumi’s Persian poetry explores love, spirituality, the human condition and the search for truth.

{{< /iran-book >}}

{{< iran-book title="Iranin mattoja – Iranian Carpets" img="img/blog/books/iranian-carpets.jpg" query="Iranin mattoja Iranian Carpets Pirjo Varjola" label="Search Vaasa Library and check reservations" >}}

Published in connection with the Museum of Cultures in Finland, this book explores Persian carpets through their patterns, craftsmanship and regional traditions. Carpets are both everyday objects and important expressions of Iran’s artistic heritage.

{{< /iran-book >}}

{{< iran-book title="Háfez: Ruusu ja satakieli" img="img/blog/books/ruusu-ja-satakieli.jpg" query="Háfez Ruusu ja satakieli" label="Search Vaasa Library and check reservations" >}}

Another Finnish selection of Hafez’s poetry, translated by Jaakko Hämeen-Anttila. Its title, *The Rose and the Nightingale*, evokes two classic Persian poetic symbols: the nightingale as the lover, and the rose as the beloved or beauty.

{{< /iran-book >}}

{{< iran-book title="Persian Painting" img="img/blog/books/persian-painting.jpg" query="Persian Painting Basil Gray" label="Search Vaasa Library and check reservations" >}}

Basil Gray’s *Persian Painting* is an illustrated introduction to Persian painting and manuscript art. It highlights intricate detail, vivid colour and the rich storytelling tradition found in illustrated Persian manuscripts.

{{< /iran-book >}}

## Fal-e Hafez: a wish, a poem and a moment of reflection

Visitors could also try **Fal-e Hafez**, a longstanding Iranian cultural tradition. A person thinks of a wish or question, opens the collected poetry of the 14th-century Persian poet Hafez at random, and reflects on the poem as a symbolic response. The practice is especially popular at Yalda Night and family gatherings. Some people find spiritual guidance in it; others enjoy it as a poetic way to reflect rather than a literal prediction of the future.

**How to try it:** Think of a wish or question, choose a fortune at random, then read and reflect on the poem. You do not need to share your wish with anyone.
